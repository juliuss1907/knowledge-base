# You Need a Mindset Shift on Priorities

**Author:** nqdonzo
**Source:** Substack
**Published:** ~2026-07

---

Analysis paralysis from too many tasks is detrimental to momentum. The antidote: mental models, specifically the Pareto Principle.

## Origins

- **Vilfredo Pareto (1906):** Italian polymath noticed 80% of Italy's land owned by 20% of population. Also observed 20% of plants bearing 80% of fruit in his garden.
- **Joseph M. Juran (1941):** Romanian-born American engineer applied the concept to quality management — 80% of problems stem from 20% of causes. Coined "the vital few and the trivial many."

## The Principle

80% of outcomes come from 20% of causes or inputs.

> "The top 20% of your priorities will give you an 80% return if you spend 80% of your time on the top 20% of your priorities." — John Maxwell

## Non-linear Input/Output

In hunter-gatherer days, inputs = outputs (linear). But modern inputs are non-linear:
- 20% input (coding a backend for a few days) → 80% output (revenue, connections, attention)
- Asymmetry is staggering

## The Flip Side: 80% Effort → 20% Results

- **Busywork disguised as productivity:** planning to plan, over-consuming information, doom scrolling, redundant errands, pointless organizing, over-formatting, unnecessary meetings
- Knowledge without execution yields nothing
- "You read a ton of articles, take notes, watch lectures, convincing yourself that you'll be ready when you finish a certain amount. All of this is high effort — but are you yielding any progress? No."

## Key Takeaways

1. Identify your most high-effort tasks. If they yield little progress, cut them out
2. Do the few hard tasks first — then easy tasks become a cakewalk
3. Simplification scales — "identify, then cut out"

## Practical Examples

| 20% → 80% | 80% → 20% |
|---|---|
| Fix damaged products → conversion rates rise | Unnecessary business meetings → no revenue, no networking |
| 15 min daily Substack engagement → algorithm boost | Overconsuming info → never writing, "mental dust" |

## Results of Consistent Application

- Priorities become clear
- Productivity increases
- Workloads become manageable
- You become more focused

**Bottom line:** Identify the vital few inputs. Execute those first. Cut out the trivial many.
