# After The Heater: The Rule That Keeps You Alive

**Author:** Glitch  
**Published:** June 2, 2026  
**Source:** Substack — glitchspx  
**URL:** https://glitchspx.substack.com/p/after-the-heater-the-rule-that-keeps

DISCLAIMER: This article is for educational and informational purposes only. Nothing here should be taken as financial advice. Trading involves risk, and every trader should do their own due diligence, manage risk independently, and make decisions based on their own plan.

There is one rule I keep coming back to in trading:

**After a heater, slow down.**

Not because the opportunity is gone forever.  
Not because you suddenly become bearish.  
Not because you are scared.  
But because the market has a very specific way of punishing traders after they get paid.

The market gives, then it takes back, and if you do not know when to dial back, it usually takes back more than it gave.

That is where most traders break. They are not destroyed because they can never find a good trade. They are destroyed because they find a great trade, make a lot of money, feel invincible, press too hard immediately after, and give it all back before the next real opportunity shows up.

The game is not just knowing when to go hard. The game is knowing when to stop going hard.

## The Market Has Seasons

There are times when the market is clean. Setups are respecting levels. Breakouts are following through. OTE entries are holding. Targets are hitting. Risk is being rewarded.

When that happens, you have to take advantage. That is when you press. That is when you size up. That is when you trust the edge.

But those windows do not stay open forever.

The same market that gave clean setups can quickly become choppy, emotional, overextended, headline-driven, or liquidity-starved. When that happens, the trader who keeps swinging with full size because they are still high off the last win is usually the trader who gives it all back.

The market is all about cycles. When to be aggressive and when to be patient. When to press and when to protect. When to take the layup and when to do nothing.

The problem is that most traders only study how to enter. They do not study how to come down from a win.

## The Post-Win Danger Zone

The most dangerous version of a trader is not always the trader who is down bad. Sometimes it is the trader who just made a lot of money.

After a big win, your brain changes. You start thinking faster. You start seeing setups everywhere. You feel like you are in sync with the tape. You assume the next trade will behave like the last trade. You start justifying size that you normally would not take. You begin treating unrealized opportunity like guaranteed profit.

That is the danger zone.

Because the market does not care that you just made money. The market does not owe you continuation. The market does not care that you are "playing with house money."

That phrase alone is dangerous. It is not house money. It is your money once you earned it.

If you do not mentally transfer gains from "market money" to "my capital," you will give yourself permission to lose them. That is how a $50,000 win becomes a $10,000 week. That is how a $100,000 heater becomes breakeven. That is how a life-changing month turns into psychological damage.

## My $MSFT Example

This week, I made over $250,000 on MSFT calls.

The important part is not the number. The important part is what happened after.

I sold all of it.

I did not sit there and tell myself: "This is going to keep going forever."
I did not immediately roll the entire gain into the next setup.
I did not let one great trade convince me that every trade deserved the same size.

I took the win. Then I scaled down.

Any future trades after that were fractional compared to the original size.

Why? Because once the market pays you that much, the first job is no longer to maximize every last dollar. The first job is to make sure the win stays real.

If the market kept giving opportunity, great. I could still participate. But if the tape got sketchy, if the setup quality dropped, if the market flipped, I had already locked in the majority of the move.

That matters.

Because if I lock in two-thirds of the gain and only risk a smaller piece on follow-up trades, I am still in control. I still have capital. I still have confidence. I still have dry powder. And most importantly, I am still mentally clean for the next real setup.

## The Goal Is Not To Win Every Trade After The Big Trade

This is where people get it wrong. After a big win, they try to keep the emotional high going. They want the next trade to be another monster. They want the next day to feel like the last day. They want to compound immediately.

There is nothing wrong with compounding. But forced compounding is one of the fastest ways to destroy a great week.

The goal after a big win is not to immediately hit another big win. The goal is to avoid giving the first one back.

That sounds simple, but most traders cannot do it. Because protecting gains feels boring. Sizing down feels weak. Taking fewer trades feels like you are missing out.

But this is the difference between a trader who survives and a trader who just gets lucky in bursts. Anyone can have a heater. Not everyone can keep one.

## The Heater Rule

Here is the rule:

**After a major gain, you do not get to trade the same way immediately after.**

You need a cooldown protocol.

For me, that means:
- Lock in a meaningful portion of the gain
- Reduce position size
- Stop forcing continuation trades
- Only take A+ setups
- Avoid emotional revenge entries if the next trade loses
- Keep enough dry powder for the next real opportunity
- Withdraw gains and reduce your portfolio size

The exact percentages can vary by trader. But the concept does not change.

If you just made a large amount of money, your next trade should not automatically be the same size. Your next trade has to earn that size. The market has to prove it is still clean. The setup has to prove it is still worth pressing. Your mental state has to prove it is still disciplined.

If those things are not true, you size down.

## Dry Powder Is A Position

One of the most underrated positions in trading is cash.

Not because cash is exciting. Because cash gives you optionality.

If you give back your heater immediately, you are not just losing money. You are losing the ability to attack the next clean opportunity. That is the part most traders underestimate.

They think the loss is only the dollars they gave back. It is not.

The real loss is lost capital, lost confidence, lost clarity, lost flexibility, and lost ability to size when the next real setup appears.

The market will give another opportunity. It always does. But if you are emotionally damaged and financially overextended when it comes, you will not be able to take it properly.

That is why preserving capital after a heater is not defensive. It is offensive. You are protecting your ability to strike again.

## When To Go Hard

There are times to press. You should not be timid when the market is clean and your edge is present.

Going hard makes sense when:
- The setup is high conviction
- Risk is clearly defined
- The market is supporting the trade
- The target ladder is open
- The entry is near OTE or a clean retest
- You are mentally neutral
- The position size is intentional, not emotional

That is when you attack. That is when the account can grow. That is when you lean in.

But the key is that aggression has to be earned by the setup. It cannot be earned by your last P&L screenshot.

## When To Dial Back

You dial back when:
- You just had a massive win
- You feel euphoric
- You start seeing too many setups
- You want to trade because you are excited, not because the setup is clean
- The market starts getting choppy
- Your first target has already been hit
- You are entering late
- You are sizing based on recent profit instead of current risk
- You feel annoyed that you are not in something

Those are warning signs. Not because the next trade cannot work. It can. But the odds of emotional execution go way up after a big win. That is why size has to come down before discipline breaks.

## The Market Gives And Then Tests You

A heater is not just a reward. It is also a test.

The market gives you money and then watches what you do with it. Do you protect it? Do you respect it? Do you slow down? Do you stay disciplined? Or do you immediately start acting like the market owes you more?

This is why so many traders repeat the same cycle: They build. They press. They win. They overpress. They give it back. They get emotional. They go on tilt. They blow up. Then the next perfect setup comes and they have no capital left.

That is the part I care about avoiding.

The goal is not to be max size all the time. The goal is to be max size at the right time. And to be small enough during the wrong time that you are still alive for the next right time.

## A Practical Framework

After a major win, I think every trader needs a simple framework. Mine looks something like this:

1. **Pay Yourself First**
Take the win out of the emotional trading bucket. Once gains are locked, they are real. Do not mentally treat them as fuel for reckless follow-up trades.

2. **Reduce Size Immediately**
The next trade after a heater should usually be smaller. Not because you lack conviction. Because your emotional state is not neutral yet.

3. **Trade Only The Cleanest Setups**
After a big win, your filter should get tighter, not looser. You do not need to trade everything. You need to protect the week.

4. **Avoid Late Entries**
The worst thing to do after a big win is chase an extended move because you feel like you are supposed to keep winning. If the OTE is gone, wait. If the retest never comes, wait. Missing a trade is better than donating back a heater.

5. **Keep Dry Powder**
The next cycle will come. The next A+ setup will come. The next moment to press will come. Your job is to still have capital when it arrives.

## The Real Flex

The real flex is not making a lot of money on one trade. The real flex is keeping it.

The real flex is being able to say: "I got paid, I protected the win, and I am still ready for the next opportunity."

That is what separates traders who survive from traders who only have screenshots. Screenshots do not matter if the account gives it all back. P&L spikes do not matter if the equity curve always round trips.

The goal is not a highlight reel. The goal is progression.

## Bottom Line

When the market is giving, press.
When the market pays you, respect it.
When you are on a heater, slow down.

Do not let one great trade turn into ten forced trades. Do not let confidence turn into entitlement. Do not let profit turn into fuel for bad decisions.

Lock in the win. Dial back size. Wait for the next clean setup. Keep dry powder.

Because the market will give again. But only the traders who protect themselves after the last heater will be ready to go hard when the next one comes.
