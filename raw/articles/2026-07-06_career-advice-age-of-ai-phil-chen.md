# Career advice in the age of AI

**Author:** Phil Chen (@philhchen)
**Source:** X Article
**Published:** 2026-07-02
**Engagement:** 5,668 likes · 796 RT · 108 replies · 17,631 bookmarks · 2.9M views

---

AI models get better at anything you can write a loss function for, and school is mostly loss functions: well-defined problems graded against known answers. Therefore, the valuable work of the next decade is everything that can't be graded within the span of model training.

Phil Chen has worked at: his own startup → Helm AI (15→50 FTE) → Scale AI (500→1500 FTE) → OpenAI (1500→3000 FTE) → Google (100,000+ FTE). As a founder of an agent-native company, he has a unique perspective on hiring for the AI era.

---

## 1. Focus on resources that are truly limited

Before Scale, Phil had quant offers with higher guaranteed cash but chose Scale for the community and exposure to LLM inference providers — which led to DeepMind and OpenAI opportunities.

> "Today, the unique network and learning opportunities from Scale have contributed far more to my life than the extra cash I would have received from quant."

Access to capital is easier than ever. Access to **time** and **strong relationships** with other humans is still rare. **Proven excellence** in past endeavors remains the highest signal.

> "Relentlessly prioritize your time so that whatever you work on, you focus on problems that you find meaningful. With vibe-coding, it's easy to find opportunities that turn a quick buck, but the prize is usually much larger when you search for real value."

**Key limited resources:** Time, relationships, and reputation.

---

## 2. Learn to find problems in addition to solving them

At his agent-native company, traditional Leetcode-style questions and system design questions feel uncorrelated with actual job performance. Their interviews measure: how well someone can **understand the environment**, **identify problems worth solving**, and **execute under existing constraints**.

> "The most important skills will be the ones related to problem selection and resource allocation."

Great candidates bring **high-level intuition** and **outside context** to their collaboration with agents. They've immersed themselves in problem-solving environments — either from passion projects or high-growth companies where meaningful problems outnumber the people.

---

## 3. Work on the most ambitious form of a problem

The "bitter lesson" framework: scaling general methods ultimately outperforms task-specific optimizations. This applies to choosing problems and companies.

> "Real, durable value only gets built with extreme focus on truly ambitious problems."

To evaluate a company: is it working on the **most ambitious form** of their problem, and do they actually have a shot at solving it?

To evaluate a role: will it allow you to work directly on the **frontier** of whatever problem the company is solving?

---

## 4. Sprint the last mile

Alfred Lin: the last 10% is both 90% of the work and 90% of the reward.

AI has polarized outcomes because the median result is what an agent can produce with a sloppy prompt. Value therefore comes from providing a **unique perspective** or **attention to detail**.

> "Learning to execute well in the last mile requires both practice and focus. Nothing is perfect on the first try, so the last mile is often about iteration."

Because progress with coding agents is rapid, it's often better to take learnings from prior iterations and **start from scratch** with the next generation of intelligence.

---

## 5. Increase both xG and efficiency

In soccer, **xG** (expected goals) measures how many goals a team is expected to score based on opportunity quality. Efficiency is conversion rate.

Phil's xG moments:
- Turned down Anthropic (~50 FTE) and Cursor (2 non-founder FTEs) in 2023 for DeepMind
- Turned both down again in 2024 for OpenAI
- Each would have been high xG from a career perspective, but he chose alignment with interests and culture fit

> "I don't believe that ASI will replace all humans in knowledge work jobs because humans have differential capabilities in selecting meaningful problems for ASI to solve, and in allocating capital to solve these problems."

**How opportunities come:** The Cursor opportunity came because of good reputation among mutuals. The Anthropic opportunity came from investing professional and personal time into problems interesting to their team.

> "At its core, selecting early-stage companies is primarily about the team and the market. Many candidates today anchor onto the existing product, but that almost always evolves into something very different if the team is good. Anthropic's initial demo was a Slackbot that was worse than ChatGPT for me."

---

## 6. You can break into research now

Modern research is easier with more compute. Start by using models and distilling your own intuitions into evaluations. Public optimization leaderboards provide structured settings for exploring ideas.

> "Ultimately, I believe that being a researcher is a mentality, not an occupation."

Being a researcher in frontier labs = curious exploration + fighting infrastructure to implement ideas + understanding the full system in detail to debug efficiently + articulating value to secure more compute. **You can do all of this without being in a frontier lab.**

---

## Closing thoughts

> "The world is still full of opportunity. The key to unlocking them is to focus on finding interesting problems and delivering extraordinary results."
