## Summary

Based on Charlie Munger's concept of "Latticework of Mental Models" (Warren Buffett's partner): Mental model = a concept explaining how the world works. Examples: natural selection (biology), critical mass (physics), margin of safety (engineering). Munger believed that to have good judgment in investing, you need a network of mental models from many disciplines, not just finance.

---

## Core Ideas

### Multi-disciplinary

**Principle:** Mental models must come from physics, biology, psychology, engineering — not just finance.

### Avoid "Physics Envy"

**Principle:** Don't force complex systems into one simple formula.

### Deep Understanding > Memorization

**Principle:** Memorization is less valuable than true internalization.

### The Danger of Few Models

**Principle:** If you only have 1-2 models, you'll bend reality to fit them.

> "If you only have one or two models, the nature of human psychology will cause you to torture reality until it fits your model — or at least you think it does." — Charlie Munger

---

## How Munger Applies This

- Embrace the complexity of investment problems
- Prepare with staggering degree of preparation
- Reduce problems to the most basic, emotionless elements
- But don't reduce to rigid Newtonian formulas

> "What I'm against is excessive self-confidence, thinking that you know for sure that your actions will do more good than harm. You're dealing with extremely complex systems where everything interacts."
