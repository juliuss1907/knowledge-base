# Happiness is a skill (here's how you can master it)

**Author:** Hussain Ibarra  
**Source:** Substack  
**Published:** 2026-07-16

## Personal Story: From Suicidal to Seeking Happiness

The author tried to commit suicide at age 8 due to bullying and having no friends from kindergarten through 2nd grade. Life effectively began at age 13. While no longer suicidal, he still struggles with depressive cycles lasting 3-16 weeks — feeling empty, no joy, no motivation, constant brain fog and negative thoughts.

After getting tired of this cycle, he spent 2 months researching the science behind happiness, exploring spirituality, religion, faith, and scientific research.

## Goals of This Essay

1. **Primary:** Provide a guide to improve quality of life and experience enjoyment even in activities not typically considered "fun"
2. **Secondary:** Help understand where misery, pain, and suffering come from, how to overcome them, and why chasing pleasure doesn't improve lives
3. **Process:** Show a 5-step process to maximize happiness, find meaning, purpose, and fulfillment

**Key reference:** Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi and his book *Flow*

---

## Happiness is Not an Object

If happiness were an object, you'd get the same happiness every time you engaged with it. But you can binge-watch your favorite show and feel nothing. You can get the date, promotion, money — and still feel empty.

This is **hedonic adaptation**: your mind's ability to drop to baseline after positive or negative experiences.

**Implication:** If you keep outsourcing happiness by chasing external results, you'll spend a lifetime living a life you never signed up for.

> "How we feel about ourselves... ultimately depend directly on how the mind filters and interprets everyday experiences."
> — *Flow*

---

## Consciousness is Awareness

**Best definition (from psychologists):** Consciousness is awareness — being aware of your thoughts, behavior, attention, your surroundings, everything that goes on in your life, being aware that you are aware, and actively directing attention to what you desire.

**Goal of consciousness:** Take in information from external and internal worlds, evaluate it, and prioritize tasks accordingly.

Without consciousness, we "know" what's going on but remain in a reactive state. With consciousness, we can create information that didn't exist before — dream, be creative, imagine a life that doesn't exist yet and bring it into reality.

> "Control over consciousness cannot be institutionalized. As soon as it becomes part of a set of social rules and norms, it ceases to be effective in the way it was originally intended to be."
> — *Flow*

---

## Attention = Psychic Energy

Attention is potential psychic energy. Without it, no work can be done. Your life is created by how you invest this energy.

Information enters consciousness because we focus attention on it. You pass hundreds of cars while driving but only remember the one that cut you off — because you focused attention on it.

> "The mark of a person who is in control of consciousness is the ability to focus attention at will, to be oblivious to distraction, to concentrate for as long as it takes to achieve a goal, and not longer."
> — *Flow*

---

## Entropy and Psychic Disorder

**Psychic entropy:** chaos, disorder, randomness in your mental system

Everything in the universe has entropy. Entropy is always increasing unless external energy maintains order.

Any information contradicting your identity or distracting from goals creates psychic entropy. Unless you catch yourself and redirect attention, you descend into chaos.

> There's no such thing as "staying the same" — entropy always increases.

The more psychic energy you invest trying to keep things the same, the less you have for new relationships, skills, promotions, hobbies.

---

## Pleasure ≠ Happiness

Most people think happiness equals ecstasy (getting high, drinking, partying, good food, sex, traveling). But **pleasure is just a quick dopamine hit**. Once it fades, you're back to baseline.

> "Pleasure helps to maintain order, but by itself it cannot create new order in consciousness."
> — *Flow*

**Enjoyment** comes from novelty and accomplishment:
- Playing a close game of tennis
- Finishing a hard video game level
- Launching a business
- Learning a new skill
- Writing about a hard subject

These activities don't feel pleasurable in the moment — they're hard and frustrating. But afterward, you wish you could do it again because it helped you grow.

**Key distinction:** You can experience pleasure without psychic investment. You cannot experience enjoyment without investing psychic energy (focus, attention, time, effort).

---

## The Socialized Person Trap

> "A thoroughly socialized person is one who desires only the rewards that others around him have agreed he should long for... He may encounter thousands of potentially fulfilling experiences, but he fails to notice them because they are not the things he desires."
> — *Flow*

Most people hate their lives because they wake up immediately filling consciousness with psychic entropy:
- "I have to go to work"
- "I'm gonna get stuck in traffic"
- "I need to deal with my sh*tty boss"
- "The tasks are so long"
- "I have a deadline"

They ruined their day before it started — while still lying in bed.

## Problems Don't Exist in the Now

Research shows happiness comes from being **one-pointed** — when all attention is directed to one thing.

Yogis spend decades mastering this through meditation. It is in that state you enter **flow state**.

**Flow state** = optimal human experience. Athletes call it "The Zone":
- Playing basketball and every shot goes in
- Painting and every brush stroke is perfect
- Writing and ideas keep coming
- Solving math problems without mistakes

Peak productivity, performance, and happiness all exist in this state.

> "The best moments usually occur when a person's body or mind is stretched to its limits in a voluntary effort to accomplish something difficult and worthwhile. Optimal experience is thus something that we make happen."
> — *Flow*

---

## What Happens in Flow State

- **Default mode network becomes less active**
- **Reward circuits activate** — gain reward from the activity itself (not outcome), helping sustain the state
- **Prefrontal cortex quiets down** — inner critic disappears, self-monitoring shuts down
- **Amygdala is less active** — less negative emotions, more positive emotions

**The opposite of flow is boredom.** When bored, your mind is distracted. You do everything to kill boredom because you don't have a goal to rewire psychic energy toward — creating psychic entropy.

---

## How to Enter Flow State

**Critical insight:** Flow state is a state of consciousness, not action. You can't *do* a state of mind. You can only facilitate it.

> You can't *make* yourself sleep. You can only go to bed, which helps you fall asleep.

**The only thing you can do:** Direct focus and attention on something for an extended period.

### 5-Step Process to Achieve Flow:

1. **Identify a problem** — feeling unhappy, unmotivated, distracted
2. **Set a goal** — understand what happiness is
3. **Dive into the unknown** — read papers, books, lectures
4. **Experiment with solutions** — meditate, journal, walk, run
5. **Distill findings into organized thoughts** — write, share findings

### Goal Structure for Flow:
- **Starting point** — know where you are
- **Desired outcome** — know what you're aiming for
- **Plan/sequence** — actions to complete
- **Rules** — understand what you're trying to do
- **Feedback system** — know if you're heading in right direction

---

## Challenge-Skills Balance

**If task is too easy → boredom**  
**If task is too hard → anxiety**

You need a challenge hard enough to keep focus but easy enough not to overwhelm.

> "The goal is to stretch, not snap." — Steven Kotler

**When task is too easy:** Add restrictions, deadlines, anything to make it harder.

**When task is too hard:** Metabolize the emotion. You're overwhelmed not by the task but by fear of future consequences.

**How to metabolize emotion:**
- Journaling
- Speaking to a friend
- Going on a walk
- **Simplest:** Center attention on breathing

### Breathing Exercise for Calm:
1. Close eyes
2. Pay attention to body proprioception (arms, head, nose)
3. Focus on solar plexus (below sternum, above stomach)
4. As you breathe, notice the **feeling of emptiness, an absence of feeling** there
5. Meditate on that absence — get closer to shunya (the void underneath ego, labels, roles, thoughts, emotions)

---

## Ego is the Enemy of Flow

In flow state, you lose sense of self, identity, and ego. You don't care what's happening *to you* — you care about what you're doing. You become one with the activity.

**What keeps ego in the picture? Comparisons.**

You cannot compare yourself to someone else without attaching ego to it. You can never compare without saying "I".

> When the "I" exists, the ego exists with it.

As long as you keep comparing, you can never be happy — there's always someone better, more attractive, wealthier, taller, more productive.

---

## Lingering Thoughts Block Flow

Even if you do everything right, **lingering thoughts** (persistent things you know you should do but avoid) keep you from flow.

**Two ways to remove them:**
1. Disappear to a mountain, meditate 20 years, achieve unknown detachment
2. **Just go and do the thing**

You won't become happier from doing it, but it helps you become one-pointed — making flow possible.

---

## Stack Good Days

You can consistently have good days:
- Eat clean
- Hit the gym
- Go for a run

Then do it again tomorrow. Momentum builds.

**Momentum goes both ways:** It propels you forward (stacking good days) or digs you deeper (stacking bad days), creating more entropy.

> You don't need to feel like doing it. Happiness comes from investing psychic energy into meaningful goals — not from pleasure.

---

## Summary: The Path to Happiness

1. **Happiness is an internal job** — stop outsourcing it to external results
2. **Control consciousness** — direct attention at will, be oblivious to distraction
3. **Invest psychic energy** — attention creates your life
4. **Fight entropy** — constantly put energy into maintaining mental order
5. **Pursue enjoyment, not pleasure** — enjoyment requires investment and creates growth
6. **Enter flow state** — one-pointed focus on challenging, meaningful goals
7. **Kill the ego** — stop comparing, become one with the activity
8. **Do the lingering things** — clear mental blocks
9. **Stack good days** — create momentum through routine
