Recently I’ve had a few conversations with friends that are feeling lost and confused about where they want to be in life. They feel a sense of unfulfillment – maybe with their career, the city they live in, the path they’re headed in. But they can’t pinpoint what’s wrong – only that something’s not right. The sense of unfulfillment creeps up on you before you realize it, and then you find yourself burnt out, stressed out, feeling like there is a life out there that you’re meant to be living, but you’re only seeing glimpses of it. As someone who’s pretty much made it my life’s mission to give myself the most fulfilling and happy life possible during my one short time on this planet, here are some thoughts.

Sometimes when you feel lost and have no clear sense of direction, the most valuable thing you can do is to decide you’re about to enter a season of life where you’ll intentionally increase the surface area for luck and serendipity to occur. Here’s what I mean by that: We’re often quite bad at predicting how things will unfold. That’s why I find it really difficult to make ten-year plans. All of the most life-changing and life-shaping opportunities, people, and connections in my life, have appeared from a side I didn’t expect. Someone inviting me to something I decide to go to last minute, where I’ll end up meeting someone who later changes my life. One conversation that leads to an unexpected idea. One message that leads to a job or an open door that shapes the direction of my life far more than I know in that moment.

I can identify so many of these moments in my past, and I credit many of the biggest turning points in my life and career to them. So I’ve thought a lot about how to have more of these moments. If they depend on chance, how can you increase the odds of it occuring? The answer is by deciding that it’s time to enter a season of your life where you will meet as many people as possible, say yes to as many things as possible, and find yourself in as many rooms as you can… Because you never know.

When you’re not sure where you’re headed, exposing yourself to as many new people and conversations as possible is often exactly what you need. By having conversations, listening to people’s stories, their unique paths, and approaching it all from a place of genuine curiosity, you learn far more about yourself than you expect. It needs to be combined with some real self-awareness and honesty with yourself. When you combine those two things, you can start gathering so much valuable information about what actually does deeply light you up. You start to notice when you feel a spark of inspiration and momentum upon hearing someone’s story or career path – and then you ask yourself what it is about that that appeals to you so deeply. Jealousy is a powerful feeling, because it can sometimes point you in the direction of what you truly want that you maybe haven’t been conscious of. You start to put together the pieces of the puzzle of where your personal fulfilment lies. You can’t do that if you only ever hear from the same people, listen to the same stories, have the same conversations.

All of this also applies to knowing when it’s time to make a change. You often know you want a change (of career, life path, relationship, city) long before you realize it consciously. The signs appear in small fragments every time you see or talk to someone living the life you want to be living – you catch glimpses of it, and the unfulfillment becomes harder to ignore. You owe it to yourself to listen to those signs. This is how you get to know yourself for real, and become responsible for giving yourself the life you want: you decide that you will not be an obstacle in your own path to fulfillment; that you will listen to every sign, and you will follow the path it is pointing towards. Change is intimidating, but what’s more terrifying is living a life wondering what would have happened if you had followed that one thing, moved to that one city, started the project, written the book.

Once you start piecing together that puzzle and the path forward appears, you owe it to yourself to follow it – unapologetically and without prioritizing other people’s expectations of where you should be heading instead. The world needs more people who wholeheartedly go after what they deeply want – not what they think they should want. The more fulfilled you are, the better you are to the world and to others, too. Your version of happiness and success may sometimes look different from that of the people around you. Without being honest with yourself, it’s very hard to figure out what it actually does look like for you, and to give yourself permission to follow that.

And what if it feels too big, too improbable, too audacious? In the dreams that feel the most audacious, that’s usually where you’ll meet a key part of yourself. If there’s one thing I’ve learned, it’s that most of the time, we underestimate our ability to achieve something if we actually go after it wholeheartedly. So why not at least try? Is anything more worthwhile than giving yourself a shot at making the most of the one life you have?
