# Play long-term games with long-term people

"Play long-term games with long-term people." — Naval Ravikant

Nearly everything good in a life—money, skill, love, reputation—comes from compound interest. And compounding has two requirements that people quietly violate every day: it needs time, and it needs the same counterparty to keep showing up.

Short-term thinking re-rolls the dice. Every quarter, every deal, every relationship is started from zero, optimized for the immediate grab, then abandoned. You can win plenty of single rounds that way. You'll never compound.

Long-term thinking does the opposite. It lets the dice keep paying out. The friend you've trusted for fifteen years is worth more than a hundred new contacts, because the trust has been compounding the whole time. The skill you've practiced daily for a decade isn't ten times a beginner's—it's a different category of thing.

This is also, quietly, why honesty wins. In a one-off interaction, cheating can pay. In a repeated game with people who'll be there for decades, your reputation carries the balance forward, and a single betrayal wipes out years of accrued trust. Integrity isn't just a virtue here. It's the optimal strategy. The long-term player is honest because dishonesty doesn't compound, it detonates.

So the real question to ask, about any game and any group of people, is: do I want to be doing this, with these people, in twenty years? If yes, lean in and let time do the heavy lifting. If no, be honest that you're playing for the short term, and don't be surprised when it doesn't compound.

The people you want are the ones who care more about the relationship than the transaction. They're rare. When you find them, the move is not to extract—it's to stay.
