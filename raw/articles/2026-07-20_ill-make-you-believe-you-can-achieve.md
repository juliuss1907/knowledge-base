and specifically in psychology, we would call this learned helplessness.

it's the state where someone continuously faces a negative, uncontrollable situation and eventually they stop trying to change their circumstances, even if they have the ability to do so.

from a subconscious standpoint? it's hard to get out of that state.

there's a lot of notes and content that preach about delusional optimism but never really go in depth on it specifically.

in short, you basically hold your self-concept on a higher level than what's objectively true.

you can be someone that believes that everything will work out in your favor, even if you don't have the physical evidence beforehand.

the underlying law that operates under this powerful level of self-belief is the law of assumption, brought about by Neville Goddard.

the main takeaway from this law is this, and pay attention.

you must assume the feeling of your wish fulfilled for it to become your physical reality.

and in layman's terms, visualize the feeling of something as if it already happened, so that reality will be forced to bend to your favor.

but that's not the most important part.

it's also important to consider taking action and executing upon having this level of self-belief.

a million dollars won't just magically materialize out of nowhere just by wishing for it.

you need to consistently show up and put in the necessary work in order for the law to take effect.

we see a lot of delusional optimists that practice this throughout history.

look at Muhammad Ali, for example, in an interview, he yelled out the infamous "I'll show you how great I am!" before fighting George Foreman.

and Ali walked in as an underdog, he had full-on confidence he would beat Foreman in the ring. and it proved true, he knocked out Foreman in the 8th round.

other athletes too practiced this law and had this insane level of self-belief.

look at Michael Jordan in his interviews, the way he interacted with reporters. look at Conor McGregor in his prime, in the way he predicted his fights before they happened. look at Kobe Bryant and his demeanor when he is ahead of the competition.

they had a level of self-belief the average person doesn't have.

they were delusional in nature, and that's quite literally the cheat code.

of course, it's important to understand that you still have a lot of room to grow and learn as an individual, to practice humility.

but to believe that you are greater than you are despite having no results to show for it yet, that's how you should operate to believe you can achieve anything with full-fledged confidence.
