# The Learning Method Charlie Munger Never Wrote Down (But Lived By)

**Author:** Flo (Undiscovered Compounders)
**Source:** Substack
**Published:** 2026-01-26

---

## Core Idea

> "I came to law school having learned the method of learning, and nothing has served me better in my long life than continuous learning." — Charlie Munger

Learning is one of the few processes we can run almost every day for the rest of our lives at very low cost with absurd upside. Based on Dunlosky et al. (2013) — a comprehensive review of learning science.

---

## The Evidence Base: Dunlosky et al. (2013)

After reviewing a large body of evidence across four dimensions (conditions, learner profile, material, criterion tasks), the paper rates techniques:

**High utility:**
- Practice testing (testing yourself)
- Distributed practice (spacing)

**Moderate utility:**
- Elaborative interrogation
- Self-explanation
- Interleaved practice

**Low utility:**
- Summarizing, Highlighting/underlining, Rereading, Imagery for text, Keyword mnemonic

Build the system around the two high-utility pillars, plug in moderate-utility modules where they pay off.

## The Full Learning System (9 Steps)

### 0. Pre-setup
Define your goal + time horizon: what do you want to know, and by when?

### 1. Define the Proof
Define what "done" looks like:
- **Concept:** Explain it with no notes + give 2 counterexamples
- **Procedure:** Redo the exercise cold, with no template
- **Domain:** Solve novel cases (not ones you've already seen)

Without this, you optimize a proxy instead of learning itself.

### 2. Break Material Into Testable Units
Convert learning material into items — discrete things to reproduce on demand:
- **Facts:** Definitions, formulas, dates, vocabulary
- **Concepts:** Relationships, causes, limits, implications
- **Procedures:** Algorithms, methods, steps
- **Discrimination:** Choosing the right method among several similar ones

### 3. Turn Every Item Into a Question
Write questions, not notes:
- **Fact:** "What is X?"
- **Concept:** "Why does X imply Y?"
- **Procedure:** "How to solve/derive/apply?"
- **Discrimination:** "In this case, which method and why?"

Over a long life of learning, your "question bank" is your main intangible asset.

### 4. Active Recall (Practice Testing)
- Zero support (notes/book closed)
- One explicit question → one produced answer, even if imperfect
- Always correct after by comparing to the right answer

The error makes the correction stick. Practice testing is the Swiss Army knife of learning: works for most people in most cases, cheap in time, effects show from hours to months.

### 5. Feedback — The Most Important Part
Weak test + strong feedback can beat strong test + weak feedback:
- Every answer gets corrected with one clear, explicit "best answer"
- Every recurring mistake becomes its own item (or sub-item)
- Retest quickly after an error (minutes to 2-3 days max)
- Feedback can be delayed — doesn't need to be instant

### 6. Space It (Distributed Practice)
Spacing > cramming for long-term retention. Biological: practice testing triggers cascades that take days/weeks to rewire neurons.

**Template:** D+1, D+3, D+7, D+14, D+30
- If easy: stretch intervals faster
- If fragile: shorten until stable

**Tool:** Anki (spaced-repetition flashcard). Goal: get the same item right multiple times across increasing intervals.

### 7. Add Moderate-Utility Modules (Optional)

**Module A — Elaborative Interrogation ("Why?"):**
- For simple facts/concepts. Identify a testable claim → ask "Why?" → answer in 3-4 sentences → get feedback.
- Limitation: high cognitive cost for limited return. Opportunity cost vs practice testing + spacing can be high.

**Module B — Self-Explanation:**
- Explain your own reasoning step by step during learning (concurrent, not retrospective).
- At each step ask: "Why is this step valid?" "What rule/principle did I just use?" "What would change if assumption X were false?"
- Limitation: limited scope (procedures/concepts), time cost, easy to slip into paraphrasing (chauffeur vs Planck knowledge).

**Module C — Interleaved Practice:**
- Alternate problem types to train discrimination.
- Limitation: only works in domains with large variety of problem types (strongest in math), literature is smaller, needs baseline competence first.

### 8. Reduce Low-Utility Techniques By Default
Rereading, highlighting, summaries, mnemonics, imagery. Use only as prep for questions, never as core of learning.

### 9. Repeat for Life
Cognitive activity at any age is associated with longer healthy life expectancy, and the association is stronger when activity is more intense.

## Key Distinction: Recall vs Recognition

- **Recall:** Answer comes to mind (requires stronger memory trace)
- **Recognition:** Spot the right answer among others (easier)

Focus on **recall** through practice testing. Recognition creates an illusion of competence.
