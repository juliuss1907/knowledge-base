# Our first heartbreaks were our parents

**Author:** Lethabo Dipudi
**Source:** Substack
**Published:** 2026-05-25

---

> "I think our first heartbreaks are our parents — because they are the first people we love without knowing love can hurt."

Before relationships. Before friendships falling apart. Before betrayal from people we chose. There were our parents — the people we loved first, trusted first, needed first.

And maybe that is what makes it hurt differently. Because as children, we do not know how to separate love from pain when it comes from the same hands.

---

## Parents are human too — but understanding their pain doesn't erase ours

Parents are human. Life may have been cruel to them first. Some were raised in survival. Some never experienced softness. Some are still carrying wounds they never healed from.

> "We grew up hearing about how difficult their childhoods were while being expected to stay silent about what they were doing to ours."

Many of us are still grieving things we cannot fully explain:

- The father who never stayed long enough
- The mother whose love came with anger we tiptoed around
- The parent who only showed affection when we achieved something
- The ones who provided financially but never emotionally
- The ones physically present but impossible to reach emotionally
- The parent we lost too early
- The parent we are still mourning even while they are alive

---

## How it follows us into adulthood

> "Some people's first experience of abandonment happened at home."

- Some learned to over-explain because they were never listened to
- Some became hyper-independent because asking for help always felt unsafe
- Some confuse inconsistency with love because inconsistency raised them

These things follow us quietly — into friendships where we fear being left out, into relationships where we beg for reassurance, into the way we apologize too much, shut down during conflict, struggle to believe people can love us gently without hurting us afterwards.

---

## Healing is not about hating

> "Two things can exist at once: you can understand your parents' pain and still acknowledge the pain they caused you."

Many of us are carrying childhoods we never properly recovered from. We just became older versions of children who learned how to survive emotionally.

> "Maybe that is why softness feels unfamiliar. Maybe that is why we hold onto people who hurt us. Maybe that is why being understood feels like love."
