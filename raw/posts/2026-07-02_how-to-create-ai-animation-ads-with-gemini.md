## the two methods

there's two ways to create AI animation ads depending on how much control you want over the final output.

the first method is importing an AI animation ad SOP directly into Claude and having it build out everything for you. this is the faster method and works well when you're trying to pump out a lot of ads quickly.

the second method is generating starting frames manually using Nano Banana Pro and then chaining them together scene by scene. this takes a bit longer but gives you way more creative control over exactly how each scene looks.

i'll break down both of them in detail so you can decide which one makes more sense for what you're trying to do.

## method 1: using Claude + Gemini Omni

this is the fastest way to go from zero to a finished animation ad. most of the thinking gets done for you and you're really just executing on what Claude outputs.

### step 1: get the AI animation ad SOP

i have a full SOP that breaks down exactly how to structure animation ads. it covers what each scene should include, how to time everything properly, what makes a good hook for animation specifically, and how to structure the whole thing so it actually converts.

### step 2: import the SOP into Claude

once you have the SOP you're going to paste the whole thing directly into Claude. tell Claude to dissect it and understand the structure completely before doing anything else.

after Claude understands the framework you're going to enter your brand details. tell it what product you're selling, who the target audience is, what style you want the animation to be in, and what the main selling points are that you want to hit in the ad.

Claude will then take all of that information and recommend different concepts based on your brand. it will give you text prompts to create your full storyline broken down scene by scene. each prompt is designed to feed directly into Gemini Omni so you're not doing any extra work translating between tools.

### step 3: generate each scene in Gemini Omni

now you take the prompts that Claude gave you and run them through Gemini Omni one by one. each prompt corresponds to one scene in your animation ad.

the reason Gemini Omni works so well for this is because it handles animation style consistency way better than other tools on the market right now. you tell it what style you want at the very start and it keeps that style locked in across every single scene you generate. so your claymation ad actually looks like claymation throughout the whole thing instead of shifting styles halfway through.

### step 4: stitch everything together

once you have all your scenes generated you need to add the finishing touches. voiceover, background music, and captions.

for voiceover i use ElevenLabs because the voices actually sound human and you have a ton of control over the pacing and tone. for background music i use tiktok and just find viral sounds related to my video you can also use because you can generate custom tracks that fit the vibe of your ad instead of using generic stock music that everyone else is using. and for the final edit where you stitch everything together and add captions i use CapCut because it's fast and has everything you need built in.

once you have the system down the whole process takes somewhere between 10 and 15 minutes per ad. and that's for a fully finished ad with voiceover, music, and captions ready to post.

## method 2: using Nano Banana Pro + Gemini Omni

this method gives you way more control over the exact look of each scene but it does take a bit longer to execute. if you have a very specific vision for how you want each frame to look this is the method you want to use.

### step 1: generate your starting frames

for each part of the animation ad you need to create a starting frame. this is the image that Gemini Omni is going to animate so it needs to look exactly how you want the final video to look.

use Nano Banana Pro to generate these starting frames. the key here is making sure each frame matches the style you want for the full ad. if you want claymation you need to generate claymation style images. if you want pixar 3D you need to generate that style. Nano Banana Pro handles stylized images really well so you can get pretty much any aesthetic you're going for.

### step 2: animate the first scene

take your first starting frame and put it into Gemini Omni along with a prompt describing the motion you want to happen in that scene. be specific about what actions should occur and how the camera should move.

generate the scene and review it to make sure it looks right before moving on.

### step 3: chain the scenes together

this is where the method gets really powerful and most people don't know about this technique.

take the last frame of the video you just generated. use that exact frame as the starting frame for your next scene. add your prompt describing what should happen in the next part of the storyline.

generate that scene.

now you have two scenes that flow together seamlessly because the second scene literally starts where the first one ended. there's no jarring cut or style shift because you're using the actual output from the previous scene as the input for the next one.

repeat this process for every scene in your ad. the last frame always becomes the new starting frame. you add your prompt for the next action. you generate. and you keep going until you have the full ad.

this is how you create long continuous animation ads without any of those awkward transitions or style inconsistencies that make AI content look amateur. every scene connects to the next because they're literally built off each other.

### step 4: add voiceover and music

same process as method 1. ElevenLabs for the voiceover, background music, and CapCut to stitch everything together and add your captions.

## which method should you use

if you want speed and you're running a lot of ads then method 1 is the way to go. Claude handles most of the creative thinking for you and you can pump out ads fast without getting bogged down in decisions about every single frame.

if you want more creative control over exactly how each scene looks and you have a specific vision you're trying to execute then method 2 is what you want. it takes longer but you have full control over every single frame and how it transitions into the next.

its ultimately up to how much creative direction and control you want vs speed. but a dialed in omni prompt can do a lot of the heavy work for the video itself (even the video editing).

## styles that work

the beauty of this system is that you can genuinely do ANY style you want. it's not limited to one specific look.

claymation has that stop motion clay aesthetic and has been working really well. pixar-style 3D is something people immediately recognize and trust. anime works for certain audiences and products. paper cutout has a handmade feel that stands out in feeds. lego works surprisingly well for certain niches. wes anderson aesthetic gives you that symmetrical pastel look.

you just specify the style you want in your prompts and stay consistent with that style throughout every scene. Gemini Omni does a good job of maintaining that consistency as long as you're clear about what you want from the start.
