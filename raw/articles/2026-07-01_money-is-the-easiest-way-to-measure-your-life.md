# Money Is the Easiest Way To Measure Your Life. But It Is Not the Best

**Author:** Mr. Stingy
**Source:** mr-stingy.com
**Published:** ~2026-06

---

VC Deedy Das's viral tweet about SF tech employees earning $500K+ feeling poor compared to $20M+ retirees from Anthropic/OpenAI/xAI sparked a conversation about how we measure success.

The unhappy ones are measuring their lives using the wrong metric: money.

## Money Is Amazing To Cover Basic Needs

Money buys food, warmth, shelter — Maslow's Physiological and Safety levels. Money is life's most useful tool for solving basic human needs.

## Money Is Helpful To Support Higher-Level Needs

- Money cannot buy friends, but it can pay for pizza and fuel to drive to your friend's house
- Money cannot buy love, but it can pay for gym/grooming/clothes
- Money cannot buy meaning in life, but it can pay for career experiments

Money is a great supporter, but the "I give money, you give what I want" model fails for human connection.

## Why We Measure Our Lives in Money

1. **Because our lives are supported by money** — "Money buys basic needs → more money = more success" — but this breaks at higher-level needs
2. **Because society tells us to** — work conversations about revenue, Forbes billionaire lists, salary as the most important contract number, consumerism, social media comparison

## ERG Theory (Clayton Alderfer)

A more modern take than Maslow's hierarchy. Three categories: **Existence, Relatedness, Growth.**

Progression/Regression: When lower needs are satisfied → effort shifts to higher needs. When higher needs are frustrated → effort regresses to lower needs.

- Rich person: easily pays for food/housing → thinks about work satisfaction + social events
- Poor person: focused on food/rent → less capacity for life purpose + creativity

Human needs are fluid — like apps on a phone with limited battery. Different seasons require different focus.

## The 5 Types of Wealth (Sahil Bloom)

| Type | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Time wealth** | Freedom to choose how, when, and with whom you spend time |
| **Social wealth** | Strength of relationships and reputation |
| **Physical wealth** | Health and how you feel |
| **Mental wealth** | Alignment to life purpose, space to reflect and grow |
| **Financial wealth** | Money and assets |

These are much harder to quantify than financial wealth. "How much does my family love me?" resists measurement.

## Use Money Less as a Scorecard, More as a Tool

Happiest people share: strong relationships, meaningful purpose (Ikigai), good health, living in high-trust societies with freedom and stability.

Money is the base of the pyramid. Still important. But once you reach a certain point, think less about it and more about the other things.

> If you're an "ambitious striver" (Dr. Arthur Brooks) — a high achiever constantly chasing more — you likely already do well with money or are on your way there. Now you know what makes a wonderful life beyond money. Money is a great accelerator. Imagine how beautiful a life it could be if you channel your money to support and accelerate the things that make life truly rich.
