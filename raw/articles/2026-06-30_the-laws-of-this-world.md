# The Laws of this World (Game Theory)

**Author:** Incentivising (@incentivising)
**Source:** Structuralist (Substack)
**Published:** 2026-06-30

---

The real divide is not income anymore. It is an understanding of the world itself. The world operates on a set of constants that no institution will teach you — not because they are secret, but because they are uncomfortable.

## The Problem: The Broken Model

The clear path from previous generations no longer exists: "If I work hard, I may get rewarded, then buy a house if I have enough money, and start a family if I meet someone."

Whether hard work translates to success depends almost entirely on **position**. Working three years in the wrong position produces far fewer results than two years of moderate work in the right position. That gap widens annually.

## Principle 1: Effort is Linear, Position is Exponential (Types of Leverage)

Economist Sherwin Rosen's paper "The Economics of Superstars" (1981): small differences in capability or position, when combined with leverage (scale, reach, reputation), produce enormous differences in outcome.

**4 types of leverage:**

- **People.** Contacts are a multitool, a failsafe, and an unfair advantage. You just need to talk more.
- **Capital.** Not just money — working capital, investments. "Liquid power" that boosts your positioning.
- **Media.** Content that reaches people you will never meet. The easiest type of leverage because it can be entirely curated.
- **Code.** Especially with AI agents, work doesn't have to be labor-intensive. The single most ignored leverage type.

> The correct question: "What compounds the fastest from this position?" — not "What produces the most output now?"

## Principle 2: Invisible Competence Is Just a Hobby (The Costly Signal)

People can only act on information they receive. Visibility is the primary source of information 99% of the time. The market cannot price what it cannot see.

**The costly signal** (Spence, 1973): An action that is expensive or challenging to fake. Examples: documented results, public demonstrations, verifiable claims with sources. They are costly because you opt to broadcast them publicly and shoulder blame for failures.

> Mere competence is not costly, nor is it visible. If you produce exceptional results, show them openly.

## Principle 3: The Market Is Indifferent to Effort

Nobody cares that you "grind" 10 hours a day. The market only rewards outcomes — the extent to which a problem was solved for someone who needed it solved. Price is determined by value to the buyer, not cost to the producer.

A person working sixty hours on problems the market doesn't urgently need will be paid less than someone working thirty hours on acute issues.

> You are the product. Ask: "What problem do I solve?"

## Principle 4: Most People Are Solving The Wrong Problem

Ericsson, Krampe, & Tesch-Römer (1993) on deliberate practice: undirected effort does not produce expertise. Whether effort compounds into skill depends on feedback and selecting appropriate problems.

Getting better at what you do is structurally useless if what you do is irrelevant. Efficiency never really mattered in the grander scheme.

For entrepreneurs: don't compete in overcrowded spaces with platitudes. Pick five values you actively wage war against, then build your philosophy and modus operandi around those.

> Mastering the wrong problem is worse than failure — it drains your energy as if you were working on something that mattered.

## Principle 5: Time Spent is a Cost. Results Are the Return

Output ≠ Outcome:

| Output | Outcome |
|--------|---------|
| An email | A decision by the recipient |
| A meeting attended | A relationship changed |
| A book read | A behavior changed |

Institutions reward outputs because they are measurable and attributable. They brute-force success because they can afford to. They spread the lie that hard work equals success — that's only true when operating within an institution, not working alone.

> Track your outcomes. Every significant expenditure of effort must be intended to produce an outcome. Effort has a habit of producing output without return, and that cost has no payoff.

## Conclusion

Your model of the market is your true enemy. You likely do not need to work harder. Working harder is always available — it never requires confronting your beliefs and directions. That is why most people choose it.
