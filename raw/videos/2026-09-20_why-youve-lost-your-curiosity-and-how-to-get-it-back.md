# Why You've Lost Your Curiosity (And How to Get It Back)

If you feel like nothing excites you the way that it used to, it is not because of your personality. There's literally a neurological shift that has happened in your brain chemistry that has stolen your curiosity. And in this video, I'll explain how it works and how to fix it. My name is Olga. Every time you binge watch Netflix, you binge watch reality TV, or you scroll on TikTok or YouTube, this is your brain's curiosity system doing exactly what it's designed to do, which is hunting for the next interesting piece of information. And it's the exact same dopamine system that drives scientists, artists, and every genuinely fascinating person that you've ever met. But the difference is that their curiosity has been aimed at things that enrich them. But your curiosity has been hijacked. It has been captured by things that drain you. But that is fixable.

First, we have to understand what is happening in your brain when you're in a state of curiosity. Scientists define it as a sort of mental hunger because your brain is constantly scanning your surroundings, the world around you, and when it detects a gap between what it knows and what it wants to know, it becomes hungry to close that gap, to get that information. For example, when you read a shocking headline and you physically cannot scroll past it, curiosity is what drives you to actually click on the article and actually read the article to obtain that information because your brain essentially treats information as essential for your survival.

But if your brain is this hungry for information, why does it only want junk? Why does it only want watching stupid videos? Like it doesn't want to read a book, it doesn't want to read an article or learn a new skill or do something more useful. And the answer comes down to one chemical, which is dopamine.

Dopamine is the neurotransmitter that is constantly present in your brain. It's a chemical that makes you do things. It basically motivates you to the point where you stand up and you do something. If you didn't have any dopamine, you wouldn't want to do anything at all. And when you have a healthy level of dopamine in your brain, the whole world feels like it's full of things worth pursuing. Questions feel worth exploring, books feel worth reading, conversations feel interesting. And the reason why these things feel so interesting to you is because you have a healthy level of dopamine in your system and you have enough dopamine to actually generate that motivational pull that is going to get you interested to pursue those things.

So if you're not feeling interested in the things that used to bring you joy, it might be that your brain is just not generating enough motivation to make those things worth pursuing. So how does it happen? Well, every time your brain detects something novel like a new notification, a new video, or a surprising headline, your dopamine spikes up. So it spikes up and then it drops. And it gives you that push of motivation to actually click on something and find out that information. ## The Fix: 3 Steps

But the good news is that it's completely reversible and the fix is extremely simple.

### Step 1: Reset Your Dopamine

Anywhere from 1 day to 7 days is going to help you reset your dopamine system. So just choose any amount of days in that range. And rules of the fast is to eliminate your highest stimulation behaviors like binge watching TV or algorithmic feeds like Twitter and Reddit and TikTok and Instagram or video games. Basically, you can still use the internet, but you have to fully eliminate those things that actually make the internet so high stimulation and always spike your dopamine. Like you can still read articles, but it would be much better if you do not go on social media, for example.

And during the fast, you have to create friction between you and those behaviors. You have to put your phone in another room while you work, turn off notifications, delete apps if you need to. And over the years, I kind of developed this habit where as soon as social media becomes overstimulating to me, I basically take my phone, I delete Instagram, TikTok, I delete Twitter, and I don't go there anymore. If there's one thing that neuroscience is unanimous on, it's that exercise is the most powerful tool for restoring your dopamine system. And people who exercise regularly, they have more natural motivation, they have more curiosity, and it happens because, loosely speaking, they have more dopamine to work with.
