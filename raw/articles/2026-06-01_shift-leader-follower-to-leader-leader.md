# Shift from Leader-Follower to Leader-Leader

## Summary

Technical expertise gets you promoted, but if you keep the technical mindset while managing, you'll create decision bottlenecks, teams that stop critical thinking, and organizations that collapse when you take vacation.

---

## Core Argument

### The Problem with Technical Expertise

Technical competence gets you promoted to leadership, but maintaining a technical-mindset-as-manager creates:
- Decision bottlenecks
- Teams that stop critical thinking
- Organizations that collapse when you're on vacation

---

## 3 Key Frameworks

### 1. Intent-Driven Leadership (instead of Permission-Seeking)

**Before:**
- Engineer: "Can I..."
- Leader: approve/reject

**After:**
- Engineer: "I intend to... because..."
- Leader: "What do you think I'm wondering right now?"

The leader's default question: "What do you think I'm wondering right now?" — not yes/no, but forces the engineer to think about the leader's perspective.

### 2. The Competence Paradox

> "Competence doesn't precede autonomy — it emerges from it."

To delegate authority, you need 2 pillars:
- **Technical Competence** — protocols: code review, pre-deployment checklist, ADRs
- **Organizational Clarity** — "thinking out loud": senior engineer explains the process they're debugging, creating a shared mental model faster than any documentation

### 3. Shifting from Knowing to Learning

> "The fastest way to improve results isn't to focus obsessively on results — it's to build a team capable of continually learning and improving."

- Normalize: "I don't know, let's find out"
- Blameless postmortems
- Budget time for exploration
