# The new comms playbook: show, don't promise

**Author:** a16z crypto
**Source:** Substack
**Published:** 2026-06-20

---

## The "Show Me" Era

For decades, tech earned public recognition for interesting ideas. MVPs, great ideas, and incredible teams no longer cut it. Since crypto has been hit hardest — with regulatory questions and headline-grabbing bad actors — people's BS meters have been heightened.

When traditional finance takes crypto seriously — BlackRock launching a tokenized money market fund, Fidelity filing for a crypto ETF, JP Morgan settling transactions on a blockchain it built in-house — the conversation changes fundamentally.

Welcome to the "show me" era.

## From Promissory Logic to Proof

For most of crypto's history, the vision WAS the product. You could launch with just a whitepaper and a token, and the press would meet you there. That dynamic has shifted due to:
- Lingering and growing skepticism of the technology (decades in)
- Entrance en masse of traditional financial institutions into crypto
- AI industry shipping tangibly consumer-ready products

Massive institutions stopped just watching: BlackRock's full embrace of tokenization, Fidelity's custody and ETF infrastructure, JP Morgan's Onyx network, Franklin Templeton's onchain money market fund. These are real products with compliance frameworks, institutional clients, and balance sheets.

The entry of TradFi at scale raised the baseline for what "serious" looks like. The starting point is no longer "What are you building?" but "What have you built, and who is using it?"

## Policy Maturation

Stablecoin legislation (GENIUS Act) passed last year, and comprehensive market structure legislation (CLARITY Act) is now ready for a full Senate vote. If CLARITY passes, founders can publicly talk about what they're building with specificity previously impossible.

## The Proof Stack

The layer of evidence that transforms a hypothetical narrative into a credible one:

### 1. Partnerships with Teeth
Not "in conversations with" — actual integrations, deployed contracts, partners willing to go on record about why they chose you. A partnership only works if it IS evidence of traction. A major institution/protocol/platform chose you over a dozen alternatives, and you can explain why.

### 2. Hard Data
Transaction volumes on mainnet (not just testnet), active wallets, revenue, retention curves. Not "growing fast" — percentages, timeframes, baselines. Journalists are doing their own onchain verification via Dune, CoinMarketCap, etc.

### 3. Real Signals of Product-Market Fit
The clearest proof of fit isn't a launch announcement — it's an organic and growing community that existed before the PR push. If your most enthusiastic users are your investors, that's a yellow flag (financially incentivized). If they found you through word of mouth, that's a story worth telling.

### 4. Coverage That Came Before the Press Push
Third-party validation, audits, independent research. The most credible proof is the kind you didn't manufacture — where someone else is telling the world there's something there.

## Sequencing: Lead With Proof, Not Vision

Lead with the data point you're most confident in, even if it's small:
- 1,000 active daily users who didn't know the founders > $1M in strategic backing
- $50M in volume in first 90 days > "will" process volume once it scales

Be more precise: "We're building the future of payments" is a thesis. "We've reduced cross-border settlement time from three days to four minutes, and here are three businesses using it today" is a proof point carrying a thesis inside it.

The story should emerge from the facts, not the other way around.

## Two-Track Communications

The best crypto communications operate on two tracks simultaneously:
1. Here's what we've built
2. Here's why it's the beginning of something much bigger

The proportion has flipped. In 2021: 80% vision, 20% substance. Today: the ratio is reversed.

The vision still matters — it's what makes proof points more meaningful — but that vision has to be earned by the substance underneath it.

## Bottom Line

The Show Me Era isn't a temporary correction. The growing sophistication of the crypto audience (media, institutional, retail) has permanently ratcheted up. If you have real traction, real data, and real partners, the higher bar works in your favor — it filters out noise and makes your signal louder.

The question is whether your communications strategy is built to show proof, or still built to promise it.
