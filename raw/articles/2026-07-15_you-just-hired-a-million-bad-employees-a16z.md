# You just hired a million bad employees.

**Source:** a16z.news (Substack)  
**Published:** 2026-07-14  
**URL:** https://www.a16z.news/p/the-next-ai-goldrush-tokens-loops

## The Paradox: Humans Are Now Cheaper Than Software

AI was supposed to replace human labor. It did the opposite.

**For the first time in history, humans are cheaper than software.**

Token spend per employee at top firms has exploded — AI is creating more jobs than it eliminates.

## The Railroad Parable (1841)

In the 1830s, railroads drove massive infrastructure buildout — American track mileage 120Xed in a decade. Then the system broke.

On October 5, 1841, two trains fatally collided due to coordination failure. As complexity grew, individual conductors weren't enough. Railroad companies began hiring managers, defining roles, establishing hierarchies. **Modern management was born.**

Rail became among the world's first billion-dollar industries, at its peak representing ~60% of the stock market.

**We just gave every employee unlimited headcount and budget.** Managing AI is harder than managing people because AI scales dysfunction instantly.

## The 7 Parallels Between Agent and Human Workforces

### 1. The Tokenmaxxing Hype Cycle

The tokenmaxxing hype ran its course in under a month. But token spend was never the real problem.

**People spend so much on tokens because they don't know how to use them.**

Maybe 1 in 100 employees knows how to give AI context. Loops in Claude Code, Copilot, etc. are bandaids for the fact that almost nobody can successfully prompt. Loops are brute force compensation for human inadequacy.

**You are spending tokens on spending tokens.**

### 2. The Looping Problem

Most companies today are mismanaged. The vast majority of workers don't meaningfully impact the business — they're cogs stamping approvals, hiring more cogs to fuel a machine that exists to exist.

**They're looping.**

Elon cut 80% of X's staff and the company performed better. **Just like 80% of employees do nothing, 80% of tokens today do nothing.**

People create more people. Tokens create more tokens. Looping is the new empire building.

### 3. The Broken Promises of AI

The promise of software was: build once, run forever at low cost, never supervise. AI broke that promise.

As soon as software could do anything, it couldn't do any one thing predictably. When you see tokens as employees, the promises break down:

- *"Tokens are more accurate than humans"* — only when prompted correctly
- *"Tokens are faster than humans"* — yet speed means nothing across 100 retries
- *"Tokens don't play politics"* — but they build empires of token spend
- *"Tokens don't quit"* — but they die between new model releases and sessions
- *"Tokens can be trusted"* — yet they fail confidently with perfect formatting

The one place AI really beats humans: **scalability**. Scaling humans burns enormous energy across recruiting, onboarding, attrition. Scaling tokens is instantaneous — which is why mismanaging them is so expensive.

### 4. The 100X Token

**The 10X engineer built the last era. The 100X token will build the next.**

Just as a handful of employees make others 10X more productive, some token context can cut AI effort by orders of magnitude. **There exist tokens that give 100X leverage.**

Humans are cheaper than tokens on average, but good tokens are cheaper at scale. Management converts one into the other.

### 5. The Political Problem

**Employees don't want to teach AI systems their secret sauce.**

At Meta, stock-owning employees are outraged the company uses employee context as training data. Tribal knowledge has been job security for centuries. AI asks workers to hand it all over at once.

**Nobody trains their replacement for free.**

The people who hold the 100X tokens have the least incentive to surrender them. Firms are wired to reject the most important technology for their future.

### 6. Evals as the Breakout Mechanism

The one breakout AI use case that escaped politics: **coding**. It expanded the pie and made engineers better.

The mechanism: **evals**. 99% of AI revenue today is coding because coding has built-in evals. Code runs or it doesn't.

Broader cross-domain AI use cases will only come online when someone builds the requisite evals. **Specific evals matter more than teaching employees to prompt.**

The real work of managing is turning fuzzy human processes into code, expressing the qualitative as quantitative.

**A firm's eval suite will become its most valuable resource.**

Just like OKRs are key to leveraging human workforce, evals will be key to leveraging an infinitely scalable token workforce. Evals are the path to running 100X tokens.

No two firms will have the same eval set. Evals will be key to competitive advantage. An organization running generic evals or agents has no edge.

### 7. AI Transformation Companies

Enterprises have been buying foundation model commits, application layer, and internal builds for years. All of it conceals a brutal truth:

**Nobody has AI working reliably yet.**

Silicon Valley is so convinced of this failure that its latest obsession is betting *against* today's business. "Neofirms" or "AI Native Services" startups are being funded to capture $21 trillion of services spend on the theory that incumbents will never manage the transition.

But the greatest AI assets still sit inside incumbents: differentiated processes that work, scalable through distribution channels that exist.

**"AI transformation companies" will be 10X larger than any neofirm.**

There's a Jevons paradox at work: every use case adopted surfaces ten more. The more AI-enabled a firm becomes, the more transformation it consumes. Ongoing AI transformation may become the only way to compete.

Consider Palantir — on paper the most Claude-disruptible company. By SaaS logic, $PLTR should be zero. It's not, because Palantir was never selling software. **It was selling transformation.**

In an AI-first world, transformation is more than ontologies and custom software. The real work is in the evals, token minimization, understanding a business so deeply you can program it.

**Encoding each firm's nuances into agents may become the largest economic task of the decade.**

## Conclusion

Every phase of the AI boom had its guiding cliché:
- "Sell pickaxes during a gold rush" → we built infrastructure
- "Service-as-a-Software" → we built neofirms

We have enough infrastructure. We have enough services. **Now the work is making the trains run on time.**

It's time to survey the enterprise: find the 100X tokens, record the loops that work, and direct intelligence that is being massively wasted.

**Humans just became cheaper than software.**

**Someone still has to tell them both what to do.**
